Executive Summary

After the deck↔app consolidation shipped, the live tool's two workshop presets were not presentation-ready: the daily graph was three dead-flat horizontal lines, the multi-year projection was buried at the bottom of the page, and the controls felt unresponsive. This revision fixed all three in three phases.

Workshop presets now render realistic load, solar, and FMP curves (with FMP deliberately staying below strike for Scenario 1 and above strike for Scenario 2 to preserve the deck's teaching contrast); the multi-year projection moved to a full-width section directly below the daily graph; and a diagnosis confirmed the "controls don't update" issue was a visibility problem, not a bug — resolved by the first two fixes plus explanatory notes. Critically, the monthly 5-line settlement bill remains decoupled from the chart and stays exact to the deck (e.g. Scenario 1 CKH 9,063,196,000 VND; Scenario 2 CKH 18,828,262,400 VND). Verified by 43 passing tests, a clean build, a live browser check, and deployed to production.

Background & Objective

The DPPA CFO Calculator is the interactive companion to a facilitator deck used at an in-person factory workshop in July 2026. A prior effort added two "Workshop" presets so a presenter could flip between the deck's worked scenarios and the live tool with matching numbers. On review, the presenter found the workshop view visually useless (flat lines), the headline long-run economics hard to reach, and the controls seemingly inert. The objective: make the workshop presets presentation-ready without breaking the hard-won penny-for-penny numeric parity with the deck.

Inputs & Scope

  • Out of scope: the deck .pptx and deck↔app numeric parity; the 3 existing curve scenarios; new load/solar input controls; multi-year computation logic (placement only).

Assumptions & Constraints

  • Illustrative chart, authoritative bill: the workshop chart teaches daily shape; the monthly 5-line bill (from fixed monthly volumes) is the deck-exact number. Per-hour figures need not sum to the bill — a caption states this.
  • Side-of-strike FMP: the workshop FMP curve is bounded by the midpoint↔strike gap so it varies but never crosses strike while the midpoint stays on the correct side.
  • Parity preserved: the bill keeps using fixed monthlyVolumes + marketPrice, so chart realism cannot change settlement totals.

Methodology

Work followed brainstorm → plan → phased implementation, each phase verified and committed before the next. The "controls inert" concern was diagnosed directly in the source (the Chart.js update path was already correct) before committing to a fix, avoiding a wasted bug hunt. Each phase ended with the full test suite and an HTML report; the final phase added a live browser check and production deploy.

PHASE-01 — Curve realism

Replaced flat workshop profiles with realistic load step-curves and solar bells, and added buildWorkshopFmpCurve(midpoint, strike, side) — a daily FMP curve centered on the market-price slider but bounded so it never crosses strike. Result: W1 FMP ≈ 1,092–1,225 (below strike 1,250); W2 FMP ≈ 1,542–1,680 (above strike 1,500). +2 tests.

PHASE-02 — Layout & feedback

Moved the multi-year projection from the page bottom into the focus column, directly under the daily-graph row. Added a control-effect note and an illustrative-vs-authoritative caption on the bill. DOM-order assertions added to the shell test.

PHASE-03 — Verify & ship

43/43 tests, clean build (250 KB / 82 KB gzip), live browser check (Workshop 2 bill CKH 18,828,262,400 with the −800M CfD; switching to a curve scenario clears the bill; multi-year in the focus column; zero console errors), then deployed.

Risks / Open Questions

  • Dragging the market-price slider across strike on a workshop preset compresses the FMP curve toward the strike margin rather than crossing it — an intentional guardrail, but an extreme manual value will look slightly flatter.
  • Per-hour chart figures are illustrative and do not sum to the monthly bill by design; the caption mitigates confusion but presenters should be briefed.
  • No open questions remain from the plan.
