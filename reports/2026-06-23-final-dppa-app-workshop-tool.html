Executive Summary

The app entered this plan cycle with stale, internally contradictory defaults (retail 2,100 in code vs 1,833 in docs vs the correct 2,204 in the 2025 EVN decision) and no multi-year view. Workshop facilitators had to switch between a live app showing a single synthetic day and a static 57-slide deck to make the lifetime economics argument.

All four plan phases executed successfully in one session. The app now:

  • Uses verified 2026 regulatory values across code, tests, and documentation (retail 2,204 VND/kWh from Decision 599/QD-EVN; loss factor k×K_pp = 1.0342 from Decree 57/2025; fees 523.34 VND/kWh).
  • Exposes a projectMultiYear() engine that computes per-year and cumulative BAU vs DPPA costs with independent escalation rates for EVN tariffs and strike price.
  • Shows a live multi-year panel — three Chart.js lines (cumulative BAU, DPPA, savings), four rollup pills (Year 1, 10-yr, lifetime, crossover year), and three interactive sliders — all updating without a page reload.
  • Labels FMP honestly as illustrative in four places (control label, hero copy, defaults comment, and assumptions doc) since no primary NSMO/ERAV source is available.

Recommendation: The app is ready for live use in the CEBA 2026 workshop. The one prerequisite before removing the "illustrative" FMP label is obtaining a primary-sourced average market price from NSMO or ERAV.

Background & Objective

The CEBA Vietnam DPPA workshop uses a 57-slide deck (Modules 1–6) to walk a factory CFO audience through Vietnam's two-way Contract for Difference settlement under Decree 57/2025/ND-CP. The web app was built to animate the five-line EVN bill and the FMP cancellation effect interactively — but it had grown out of sync with verified regulatory numbers and offered only a single-day view.

The brainstorm (research/2026-06-22_dppa-app-deck-consolidation-brainstorm.md) identified the primary leverage: the deck's "evaluate over lifetime, not Year 1" thesis (slide 16/30 and the Case 5/6 DSCR tables) needs an interactive counterpart that lets a facilitator move an escalation slider and watch the crossover year shift in real time. A static chart on a slide cannot do this.

Objective: Turn the single-day calculator into the live workshop engine — recalibrated, multi-year, and honest about data uncertainty.

Methodology

The work followed a four-phase sequential plan where each phase produced a testable artifact before the next began. The governing constraint was "wrapper over existing engine" — calculateSettlement was never modified; all new capabilities compose around it.

Core algorithm — multi-year projection

for year n ∈ [1 .. horizonYears]:
  retailTariff_n = retailTariff_base × (1 + evnEscalation)^(n-1)
  strikePrice_n  = strikePrice_base  × (1 + strikeEscalation)^(n-1)
  // FMP held flat
  result_n     = calculateSettlement({ ...baseInputs, retailTariff_n, strikePrice_n })
  bau_n        = result_n.totals.baselineCost × 365
  dppa_n       = result_n.totals.totalCost    × 365
  cumBau       += bau_n ;  cumDppa += dppa_n

crossoverYear = first n where cumBau - cumDppa > 0

Year 1 uses factor 1.0 — what the user sees on the sliders is Year 1 economics. Compounding starts from Year 2.

Key architectural decision: stable canvas pattern

renderAppShell creates #multiYearChart once in static HTML. Both the profile chart and the multi-year chart use a module-level instance check — if the chart already exists, call .update('none') rather than destroy-and-recreate. This keeps slider responsiveness near-instant and was the critical choice that prevented flicker on every escalation slider drag.

Risks / Open Questions

FMP primary source unresolved. The 1,427 VND/kWh figure is the most sensitive input in the model — it moves the per-kWh DPPA cost and crossover year materially. Every participant who compares the app's output to their own electricity bill will notice if FMP is off. This should be the first priority for the next research sprint.
Representative-day × 365 understates seasonal plants. A northern Vietnam rooftop with a winter dip will overperform in the app model during low-sun months. The current teaching scope is adequate; an investment-grade version needs 8,760-hour profiles.
Two-component tariff pilot risk (Oct 2025). Vietnam's two-component pilot tariff (demand charge + energy charge) could create a double-charging risk for DPPA buyers on the retail residual slice. The current app models a flat average tariff. If the pilot expands, retailTariff in the shortfall calculation will need to be decomposed.

Five-line EVN bill identity (app engine math)

EVN  = Σ matched × FMP × lossFactor       (market energy, loss-adjusted)
     + Σ matched × dppaCharge               (DPPA service + balancing fee)
     + Σ shortfall × retailTariff            (residual retail slice)
CfD  = Σ contractQty × (strikePrice − FMP)  (two-way: positive = buyer tops up)
Net  = EVN + CfD
BAU  = Σ load × retailTariff                (no-DPPA baseline)
lossFactor = k × K_pp = 1.026 × 1.008 = 1.0342
