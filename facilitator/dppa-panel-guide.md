## Tier A — run these

### Q1 · A·2 · Opener — volume vs. a long, high-risk commitment
**Question (as written):** *Major suppliers say their challenge isn't running an RFP, it's securing enough volume — today's DPPA / virtual-DPPA capacity often covers only a fraction of their target, on very long, high-risk PPAs. How should they navigate that?*
**Lead:** BUY · **Engaging:** DEV, BANK.
**Cross-seat tension:** BUY wants partial, flexible volume and short exit; DEV needs a large, long, firm offtake to finance the plant; BANK won't lend against a thin or short contract. The friction is **tenor and firmness vs. flexibility**.
**Anchor:** the three gates — buyer ≤ BAU on **10-yr & lifetime**, seller IRR 12–15%, lender **DSCR ≥ 1.20×**; and the narrow-window finding (**5 of 56** strike×volume scenarios pass all three at current prices — the computed `passCount` in `assets/teaching/gate-sweep.json`; regenerate via `cd app && node scripts/export-sweep.mjs`). Volume/tenor is exactly where the window narrows.
**Gate mapping:** buyer-≤BAU (BUY) vs. lender-tenor/DSCR (BANK) vs. seller-IRR (DEV).
**A strong answer surfaces:** start with a slice you can actually settle (consumption, not aspiration); layer additional DPPAs over time; a short PPA is often *unbankable*, so "lower risk via shorter tenor" can backfire.
**Follow-up if they agree too fast:** *"BANK — name the shortest tenor you'd finance for a tier-2 supplier, and what it costs the buyer."*
**CFO takeaway:** size the first DPPA to what you consume and can finance — not to your 2030 target.

### Q8 · A·1 · Real savings, or just hedging the next tariff hike?
**Question (as written):** *Retail tariffs rose ~17% in three years and the production cross-subsidy is being phased out. How much of the "DPPA savings" story is real saving vs. just hedging future tariff hikes?*
**Lead:** DEV · **Engaging:** BANK, BUY.
**Cross-seat tension:** DEV sells the savings narrative; BUY's CFO wants to know if it's real today; BANK treats it as a hedge, not a discount. Sharpest economic disagreement on the panel.
**Anchor:** the multi-year model — at a fixed/indexed strike, **DPPA savings *build* as EVN retail escalates ~4%/yr** against the locked strike; the "crossover" year is when cumulative DPPA cost drops below cumulative BAU. In Year 1 a deal can be *above* BAU (the deck's reference strike was ~9–14% over BAU in year 1) and still win on a 10-yr/lifetime basis. Retail basis 2,204 VND/kWh.
**Gate mapping:** buyer-≤BAU is a **lifetime** test, not a Year-1 test.
**A strong answer surfaces:** it's mostly **hedging/volatility-removal**, with real saving emerging only if retail keeps escalating above the strike's index; honest framing beats overclaiming a day-one discount.
**Follow-up:** *"BANK — if retail escalation slows to 2%/yr, does the saving survive, and does your DSCR?"*
**CFO takeaway:** judge the deal on 10-yr & lifetime cost vs. BAU — never on Year 1.

### Q18 · A·3 · Exit, suspension, and the 12-month wind-down
**Question (as written):** *Decree 243 spells out suspension/termination and a 12-month wind-down where EVN keeps buying from the generator `[verify]`. If a supplier loses eligibility or a contract lapses, what's the buyer's exposure?*
**Lead:** BANK · **Engaging:** BUY, DEV, MOD(regulator).
**Cross-seat tension:** BUY fears stranded obligations; DEV wants continuity of revenue; BANK prices termination/credit risk into the loan; the regulator's wind-down rule `[verify]` allocates who carries the gap. The panel's stated theme lives here.
**Anchor:** risk-allocation framing from the workshop's gates — a lapse hits the lender hardest (DSCR continuity). Pair with the negative-CfD reality: in a high-FMP month the **developer pays the buyer** (S2: CfD −800,000,000 on 8,000,000 kWh) — so "who owes whom on exit" depends on the FMP-vs-strike sign at the time.
**Gate mapping:** lender DSCR continuity (BANK); buyer change-in-law/exposure (BUY).
**A strong answer surfaces:** the 12-month wind-down protects the *generator's* revenue, not necessarily the buyer; put termination, step-in, and change-in-law terms in the contract, not in hope.
**Follow-up:** *"BUY — in a forced wind-down, are you still paying the CfD, and which way does it flow if FMP is above strike?"*
**CFO takeaway:** negotiate exit, step-in, and change-in-law before signing — they move the bankable price.

### Q4 · A·4 · The IP retailer — easier access, or a new middleman?
**Question (as written):** *Decree 243 added data centers and EV-charging as eligible large consumers and formally recognizes the industrial-park/cluster retailer `[verify]`. For a tenant factory inside an IP, does this make DPPA easier or add a middleman?*
**Lead:** BUY · **Engaging:** DEV, BANK, MOD(regulator).
**Cross-seat tension:** BUY (the IP tenant) gains a legal path but inherits a retailer in the middle; DEV needs a clean counterparty; BANK asks **who is the creditworthy obligor** — the brand, the tenant, or the park retailer? Recognizing the retailer can *add* a credit layer.
**Anchor:** `[verify]` 243 eligibility expansion + retailer recognition; ground the credit angle in Q11's bankability test (obligor creditworthiness).
**Gate mapping:** lender's "who's the obligor" (BANK); buyer access/cost (BUY).
**A strong answer surfaces:** the retailer makes participation *legal* but can make it *costlier and less bankable* unless the contract chain is clean; first-movers (Samsung SEVT ↔ TTC Duc Hue 2) show the structure is doable.
**Follow-up (→ Q5):** *"BUY — what one clause in your retailer contract has to change first, and who initiates it?"*
**CFO takeaway:** map your contract chain and the credit obligor before assuming the IP path is simpler.

### Q11 · A·5 · What makes a DPPA bankable?
**Question (as written):** *From a lender's view, what makes a DPPA bankable? How do you weigh buyer creditworthiness, contract tenor, and the floor price?*
**Lead:** BANK · **Engaging:** DEV, BUY.
**Cross-seat tension:** BANK wants a high floor + long tenor + strong obligor; DEV wants the high strike too (their revenue line); BUY wants the opposite on price. The buyer and lender are *aligned against* the developer on credit/tenor but *opposed* on price.
**Anchor:** **DSCR ≥ 1.20× every year** is the hardest gate; the strike floor **is** the developer's bankable revenue line; the narrow-window finding (**5/56** pass all three gates) is the blunt truth — bankability and buyer-positivity rarely coexist at current prices.
**Gate mapping:** lender DSCR ≥ 1.20× (BANK); seller IRR 12–15% (DEV).
**A strong answer surfaces:** bankability = creditworthy obligor + tenor that outlasts the debt + a floor that clears DSCR in *every* year; if the window is narrow, the fix is **structural** (BESS, volume right-sizing, indexation), not just price.
**Follow-up:** *"DEV — what's the lowest strike you could still finance, and what would have to change to get there?"*
**CFO takeaway:** your strike *is* the developer's loan covenant — push it down only as far as the lender's DSCR allows.

### Q7 · A·6 · A cost that resets every year inside a 10-year contract
**Question (as written):** *There's still no cap on CDPPA and EVN announces the figure annually `[verify]`. As a sustainability lead, how do you explain to a CFO a large cost component that resets every year inside a 10-year contract?*
**Lead:** BANK · **Engaging:** BUY, DEV.
**Cross-seat tension:** BUY needs budget certainty; BANK underwrites a cash flow with a *variable* fee component; DEV/GEN treat it as pass-through. The reset is a real uncertainty no party fully controls.
**Anchor:** the fixed DPPA fees today total **523.30 VND/kWh** (service 360 + clearing 163.30) on every matched kWh, *regardless of strike* — material but currently bounded; the risk is the annual re-set, not the level.
**Gate mapping:** lender "underwrite the variable component" (BANK); buyer budget (BUY).
**A strong answer surfaces:** model the fee as a **range** (downside/upside), not a point; build the reset into the 10-yr DSCR; prepare the documentation a lender needs to size it.
**Follow-up:** *"BANK — what reset assumption do you actually underwrite, and what document do you need from the buyer?"*
**CFO takeaway:** present DPPA cost as a banded range across years, and reserve for the annual fee reset.

### Q20 · A·7 · Closer — the biggest remaining barrier, one year out
**Question (as written):** *One year from now, what's the single biggest barrier still blocking DPPA adoption for manufacturers — regulatory, financial, or technical?*
**Format:** go-around, one word + one sentence per seat. **Expect divergence** — DEV: bankability/price; BANK: DSCR/obligor; BUY: internal approvals/tenor; MOD(regulator): procedure/243 implementation `[verify]`.
**CFO takeaway:** the binding constraint is rarely "technical" — it's the empty window between bankable and buyer-positive.

### Q21 · A·8 · Closer — the most common first-DPPA mistake
**Question (as written):** *What's the most common mistake buyers make in their first DPPA, and what would you tell this room to do differently?*
**Format:** go-around. Likely answers: over-contracting (paying terms for volume you can't settle — the workshop's excess case), judging on Year 1, ignoring the obligor/credit chain, no exit/step-in terms.
**CFO takeaway:** the three classic errors — over-contracting, Year-1 thinking, and a weak exit — are all avoidable on paper.

### Q22 · A·9 · Final closer — one 90-day action
**Question (as written):** *If each of you could name one thing this room should do in the next 90 days to move closer to clean power, what is it?*
**Format:** one concrete action per seat; moderator collects them as the audience takeaway slate. Hand to audience Q&A.

## Tier B — run if time (condensed)

- **Q5 (→ Q4 follow-up) · BUY:** *Under 243, what must change in the internal-retailer contract before signing a DPPA, and who initiates it?* `[verify]` Tension: who owns the contract amendment (tenant vs park vs developer). Strong answer: the buyer must initiate; the retailer agreement is the long pole.
- **Q13 (pairs w/ Q11) · BANK:** *How does a bank assess a tier-2 supplier's credit for energy CapEx — does a brand offtake commitment de-risk the loan?* Tension: brand wants influence without guarantee; bank wants a guarantee. Anchor: obligor creditworthiness (Q11). Strong answer: a brand *offtake or guarantee* materially de-risks; a mere "preference" does not.
- **Q14 (merge → Q7/Q11) · BANK:** *How do you underwrite a cash flow with annually-reset CDPPA+PCL and a moving FMP — what documentation?* Fold into Q7 (reset) + Q11 (bankability); run standalone only if a lender wants the technical floor.
- **Q19 · BANK/DEV:** *If the spot market is suspended, the grid DPPA settles against the most recent week's reference price `[verify]` — how worried should a buyer be?* Tension: reference-price basis risk. Anchor: FMP is the settlement reference; a stale reference shifts value between buyer and developer.
- **Q3 · MOD:** *Decree 243 amends 57 & 58 `[verify]` — what does it mean for DPPA, and your interpretation?* Use as framing context, not a debate; keep to 90 seconds.
- **Q9 · DEV:** *For a thin-margin tier-2, a realistic first step that doesn't need a 15-yr commitment?* Strong answer: rooftop/BESS or a short matched-volume slice before a grid DPPA.
- **Q10 · DEV:** *243 raised rooftop surplus sale cap 20%→50% `[verify]` — does it change the factory-roof case?* Niche/behind-the-meter; run only with a rooftop-interested audience.
- **Q15 · BUY:** *What can a brand realistically require of a tier-1, and where must it share cost/risk?* Supply-chain decarbonization; pairs with Q13/Q16.
- **Q16 · BUY/DEV:** *Who owns the RECs/EACs — supplier or brand — and how is double-counting avoided?* Distinct, ESG-relevant topic not covered elsewhere; keep if the audience is brand/supply-chain heavy.
- **Q17 · BUY:** *Which internal sign-offs (finance, legal, HSE, group HQ) most often stall a DPPA, and how do fast movers structure approval?* Best as a quick go-around; high audience value, low panel tension.
