--- Slide 4 ---
Scenario 1: The Base Case
Your manufacturing plant has a strict corporate target to reach 100% renewable energy consumption by 2030. To achieve this, your team wants to sign a DPPA via National Grid (Virtual DPPA) with a large solar developer.
Operational issue:
 Your factory doesn’t buy electricity directly from EVN. Instead, you pay your monthly electricity bill to the private Industrial Park Management Board, who buys bulk power from EVN and resells it to you through their internal grid.
Questions:
Under Decree 57, an Electricity Buyer must consume ≥200,000 kWh/month. If the IP lumps your factory into their master EVN meter, how do you accurately track and prove your specific energy use to qualify?
A standard Virtual PPA is a bilateral agreement (Between Buyer and Seller). However, since electricity delivery is controlled by the IP (Retailer), who actually needs to be at the negotiation table to make this work?
What happens if the IP demands a new, non-negotiable "infrastructure fee" to facilitate this DPPA setup? How does that impact your budget forecasting and management buy-in over a typical 15-to-20-year PPA Contract Term?

--- Slide 8 ---
Scenario 2: The "Duck Curve" & Price Crashes
Your factory agrees to a 
Virtual DPPA
 (grid-connected) with a solar developer
Fixed 
"Strike Price" of 1,800 VND/kWh.
 
Factory continues to pay local utility for the physical electricity you consume to keep the machines running. 
Factory & developer settle the difference between the Strike Price & the wholesale spot market floating price (FMP) 
Sunny month: solar energy floods 
the national grid & the midday FMP drops to 800 VND/kWh.
Questions:
Who owes money to whom? Since you are essentially paying two bills, how does this complicate your factory's monthly operational costs?
Imagine a severe heat wave causes a power shortage, and the FMP spikes to 2,500 VND/kWh. How does the VPPA financially protect your factory's budget in this situation?
If EVN raises the DPPA fees next year, your overall energy costs go up. How do you communicate this ongoing regulatory risk to your CFO for DPPA approval?
