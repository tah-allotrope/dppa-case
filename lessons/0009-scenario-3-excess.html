1 · See it move — the sunny-day clamp

FMP dips to ~930 at midday (solar floods the grid) — always below the 1,250 strike, so the CfD stays positive: the factory tops up the developer. The amber solar line above the teal load line is the excess.

3 · Where the excess goes (and why it earns nothing)

The CfD caps at consumed volume. Anything generated above consumption is sold at the spot price only — it earns no strike, for either side.

4 · Which way does the CfD flow?

FMP (1,100) below strike (1,250) → CfD positive → the factory tops the developer up — same direction as Scenario 1, a wider gap because the sunny month softens FMP.
The three canonical cases together S1 Matched: Q_c = Q_KH, line 4 = 0, CfD +500M. S2 Shortfall: Q_c < Q_KH, retail line 4 returns, CfD −800M. S3 Excess: gen > Q_KH, line 4 = 0 again, CfD +750M, and excess earns nothing. The volume axis (matched / short / excess) is the spine; the CfD sign just follows FMP vs strike.

Check yourself

1. The 1,500,000 kWh of excess generation earns ___
Excess above consumption settles no CfD — the generator sells it on the spot market at FMP only. The strike never touches it.
2. Because consumption is fully matched, line 4 (residual) is ___
No shortfall (Q_KH − Q_khc = 0) → line 4 = 0, just like Scenario 1. The excess is on the generation side, not your bill.
3. C_KH for Scenario 3 equals ___
C_EVN 8,304,644,000 + CfD (+750,000,000) = 9,054,644,000. (8.30B is C_EVN; 9.06B is Scenario 1's C_KH.)
