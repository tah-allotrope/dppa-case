Developer Economics & the Three Gates

1 · Baseline ✓ 2 · Five-line bill ✓ 3 · CfD mechanics ✓ 4 · Three gates 5 · Canonical cases 6 · Decisions

Your strike isn't just your cost — it is the developer's bankable revenue line. A deal only exists where three parties' tests overlap: buyer, seller, and lender. Sometimes that overlap is empty.

Developer revenue = Q_meter × FMP  (volatile spot)  +  (Strike − FMP) × Q_CfD  (CfD stabiliser)

1 · The CfD makes revenue bankable

A lender can't finance a volatile spot line. The CfD converts it to a near-fixed line at the strike.
Your strike IS the developer's bankable revenue. Everything the lender checks flows from it.

2 · Three gates on one strike line

Buyer pushes the strike DOWN; seller and lender push it UP. The deal lives only in the overlap.
When the lender's floor sits ABOVE the buyer's ceiling, no strike satisfies all three — the window is empty.
GateWhoTestPressure on strike
1 · BuyerYouCumulative cost ≤ BAU (10-yr & lifetime)DOWN ↓
2 · SellerDeveloperEquity IRR ≥ 12–15%UP ↑
3 · LenderBankMin DSCR ≥ ~1.20x every yearUP ↑ (hardest)

The one idea

A strike below the developer's bankability floor doesn't make a cheap deal — it makes no project. If the window is empty, the fix is structural (leverage, debt sculpting, right-sized battery, USD terms), not just price.

3 · DSCR is tested every single year

Averages don't finance a project — the worst year does. One CAPEX spike can sink it.
DSCR = cash available for debt service ÷ debt service   (needed: ≥ ~1.20x every year)
Years 1–7, 9–10 clear 1.20x — but Y8's battery replacement drops DSCR to 0.90x and the bank says no.

4 · Where the strike floor comes from

The bankable floor is built from the capital stack and a stack of "hidden" costs.
Hidden leverTypicalMoves the bankable strike floor

Why this matters to you These rarely appear in headline term sheets, but each one moves the bankable strike floor — and therefore your price. Ask for them explicitly.

Check yourself

1. The hardest gate to pass is usually the ___
DSCR ≥ 1.20x must hold every single year; one CAPEX-heavy year fails it even if averages look fine.
2. A strike below the bankability floor means ___
Below the floor the developer can't finance it, so the project doesn't happen — not a bargain, just nothing.
3. One battery-replacement year can ___
A big CAPEX spike drops that year's DSCR below covenant; lenders size to the worst year, not the average.
