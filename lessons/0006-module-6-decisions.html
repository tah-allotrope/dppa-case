The whole course in one line

Bill → CfD locks it → gates decide if a strike exists → three shapes show outcomes → you negotiate.

Fixed vs negotiable — know which is which

Don't waste breath on the fixed half. Spend it all on the negotiable half.
Fees and the loss factor are set by regulation. The strike — and how it escalates — is where the money is won or lost.

The 5 decisions to settle before you sign

Items 1–4 are quantifiable before you negotiate — run them against your load data first. Item 5 is judgment.

The one model to carry into the room

After the CfD, your matched-kWh price is just Strike + fixed fees + a tiny loss term. The FMP washes out.
This is why the strike dominates the negotiation: fees and loss are fixed, and the volatile FMP nets out.

The five takeaways

Carry these into the workshop

1 · Know the five-line bill — 523 fees and the loss factor are fixed; the strike is the big number.
2 · Evaluate on 10-year and lifetime cost vs BAU — never Year 1 alone.
3 · Every deal must pass buyer, seller AND lender gates; if the window is empty, the fix is structural, not price.
4 · Match the contract to your hourly load shape; the CfD caps at consumed volume.
5 · Negotiate the escalation index as hard as the starting strike.

Final check

1. The one decision that is pure judgment, not pre-quantifiable, is ___
Items 1–4 (strike, volume, tenor, voltage/K_pp) are quantifiable from load data; risk allocation is judgment.
2. After the CfD, your matched-kWh price is dominated by ___
FMP nets out in the CfD; only strike + 523 fees + a tiny loss term remain (~2,572 in the example).
3. If the negotiation window is empty, the fix is ___
An empty window means no strike satisfies all three gates; change leverage, debt sculpting, battery size, or FX terms.
