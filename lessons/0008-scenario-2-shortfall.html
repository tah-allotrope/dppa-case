1 · Split the consumption

Consumed 9M = matched 8M (the DPPA) + shortfall 1M (plain retail). The CfD touches only the matched 8M.
Over-consuming the contract means the gap (1M) buys at retail 2,204 — and earns no CfD.

2 · Build the bill — and watch the CfD subtract

FMP > strike makes the CfD negative, so it REDUCES your total. The developer pays you.
Two differences from Scenario 1: line 4 (retail) reappears, and the CfD is negative — it subtracts.

3 · The money flow — note the reversed CfD arrow

Generator: 12.902B spot − 0.800B CfD = 12.102B net. Factory: 19.628B EVN − 0.800B CfD = 18.828B.

4 · Which way does the CfD flow now?

FMP (1,600) above strike (1,500) → CfD negative → the developer pays the factory. This is the protection the CfD buys.
Scenario 1 vs Scenario 2 — the two CfD directions S1: FMP < strike → CfD +, factory tops up, no shortfall. S2: FMP > strike → CfD −, developer pays factory, plus a retail shortfall. Together they show the CfD is a two-way hedge — and that the shortfall always falls back to retail.

Check yourself

1. The 1,000,000 kWh shortfall is settled ___
Shortfall = (Q_KH − Q_c) bought from EVN at retail (line 4). The CfD only touches the matched 8M.
2. Because FMP exceeds the strike, the CfD is ___
(Strike − FMP) = (1,500 − 1,600) = −100, so CfD = −800M: the developer pays the factory.
3. C_KH for Scenario 2 equals ___
C_EVN 19,628,262,400 + CfD (−800,000,000) = 18,828,262,400. (19.63B is C_EVN before the CfD.)
