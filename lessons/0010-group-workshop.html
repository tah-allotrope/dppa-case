DPPA Group Workshop — Compute, then Negotiate

S1 · Matched ✓ S2 · Shortfall ✓ S3 · Excess ✓ ▶ Live exercise

You have seen the three canonical cases. Now do them. In small groups you will split into the two sides of a DPPA, compute every line of the bill by hand, check yourselves against the live tool, then negotiate a strike price and watch the CfD flip in real time. This page is your run sheet; the numbers go on the worksheets.

How the room works

Tables of 3–5. Each table splits into two roles. You will keep the same role across all three scenarios, then face a partner table for the negotiation.

🏭 Off-taker (the factory)

You buy the energy. You want the lowest all-in cost vs BAU.

  • Compute C_EVN (lines 1–4) and C_KH (after the CfD).
  • Track the effective VND/kWh and how it compares to retail 2,204.
  • Argue for a lower strike — but know the developer must stay bankable.

☀️ Developer (the RE GENCO)

You sell the energy. You want a bankable revenue line.

  • Compute plant revenue = spot (Q × K_pp × FMP) + CfD settlement.
  • Watch the CfD sign: below strike you receive a top-up; above strike you pay it out.
  • Argue for a higher strike — enough to clear the lender's DSCR (Module 4).

The one rule Compute by hand first, on the worksheet. Only open the app to check your answer. The point is to feel each line move — not to read a number off a screen.

Round 1 — Matched (Q_c = Q_KH = 5,000,000 · FMP 1,150 · strike 1,250)

Watch hours 09–15: load and solar overlap (matched), FMP sits below the 1,250 strike all day.
Your task ~18 min
  1. Off-taker: fill lines 1–4 on Worksheet S1, sum to C_EVN, then add the CfD for C_KH.
  2. Developer: compute spot revenue (5,000,000 × 1.008 × 1,150) and the CfD; sum to plant revenue.
  3. Agree which way the CfD flows and why. Note the effective VND/kWh.
  4. Verify: open dppa-case.web.app → Workshop 1. Does your C_KH match?

Reference walk-through if you get stuck: Scenario 1 lesson.

Round 2 — Shortfall (Q_c 8,000,000 · Q_KH 9,000,000 · FMP 1,600 · strike 1,500)

Load runs above solar all day (shortfall); FMP sits above the 1,500 strike → the CfD reverses.
Your task ~18 min
  1. Off-taker: this time line 4 is back — 1,000,000 kWh of shortfall at retail 2,204. Build C_EVN, then subtract the negative CfD for C_KH.
  2. Developer: spot revenue is on 8,000,000 × 1.008 × 1,600; the CfD now costs you 800M. Net plant revenue?
  3. Note the two differences vs Round 1: the retail line returns, and the CfD flips direction.
  4. Verify: Workshop 2 — C_EVN 19,628,262,400, C_KH 18,828,262,400.

Reference: Scenario 2 lesson.

Round 3 — Excess (Q_KH 5,000,000 · gen 6,500,000 · FMP 1,100 · strike 1,250)

Solar rises above load midday (over-generation); the excess 1,500,000 kWh earns the developer spot only.
Your task ~18 min
  1. Off-taker: your bill settles only on the 5,000,000 you consume — line 4 = 0 again. Build C_EVN and C_KH.
  2. Developer: compute the spot value of the 1,500,000 kWh excess — and the CfD uplift you forgo on it. Who carries the over-generation risk?
  3. Debate at the table: should the contract have been sized smaller? What term protects the over-sized side?
  4. Verify: Workshop 3 — C_EVN 8,304,644,000, C_KH 9,054,644,000.

Reference: Scenario 3 lesson.

The negotiation (~14 min)

Now face a partner table. Take Scenario 1's volumes (5,000,000 kWh, FMP ~1,150) as the starting point. Each side proposes a strike and defends it:

🏭 Off-taker opens low

A strike near or below FMP minimises your top-ups — but no bank will finance it.

☀️ Developer opens high

A strike that clears your DSCR is bankable — but a buyer below BAU won't sign it.

Settle it live
  1. Agree a candidate strike. Compute the CfD by hand: (strike − 1,150) × 5,000,000. Which way does it flow?
  2. Open dppa-case.web.app → Workshop 1, then drag the strike-price slider to your number. Watch C_KH and the sign of the CfD move in real time.
  3. Push the strike up and down. Find the point where the CfD crosses zero (strike = FMP) — and where the buyer first beats BAU on the multi-year panel.
  4. Write your agreed strike and the resulting C_KH on the negotiation row of the worksheet.

Debrief (~12 min)

Compare across tables
  • What strikes did each table land on? How wide is the spread?
  • Across S1/S2/S3, which line surprised you most — the retail shortfall, or the excess that earns nothing?
  • In which scenario did the CfD protect the factory, and in which did the factory pay in?
  • Whose gate was hardest to satisfy — buyer (≤ BAU), seller (IRR), or lender (DSCR)? (Module 4.)

Check your instincts

1. Raising the strike price moves the buyer's C_KH ___
CfD = (strike − FMP) × Q. A higher strike means a larger positive CfD (or smaller negative), so the factory pays more — C_KH rises.
2. The CfD crosses zero exactly when ___
CfD = (strike − FMP) × Q = 0 when strike = FMP. Below it the factory tops up; above it the developer pays.
