1 · One picture: the overlap and the price clamp

The same chart the live app shows — factory load, solar, matched volume, and the FMP curve crossing the strike, all on one canvas.
Left axis = kWh/h (load · solar · matched). Right axis = VND/kWh (FMP · strike). Where load & solar overlap = matched (all the CfD ever settles on); solar above load = excess (generator spot only); load above solar = shortfall (retail, line 4). Where the FMP line sits below the strike you top up the developer; at the evening peak FMP rises above strike and the developer pays you. That two-way swap is the clamp.
VND/kWh time → STRIKE — locked FMP (spot) FMP > Strike → developer pays you FMP < Strike → you top up
The two-way payments exactly offset the FMP deviation — your energy cost flattens to the strike.

2 · Two-way: who pays whom

Engine This is one line of code: developer = Q_CfD × (strike − fmp) in settlement.js. Positive = factory pays; negative = factory receives. The net energy cost lands at strike + 523 fees.

3 · Why Year-1 is a premium — and value still builds

A locked strike starts above BAU, but EVN escalates ~4%/yr and overtakes it.
Evaluate on 10-year and lifetime cumulative cost vs BAU — never on Year 1 alone.
Negotiate the index, not just the number Strike escalation (fixed VND vs CPI vs USD-linked) compounds against you for 15–25 years. Negotiate it as hard as the starting strike. Higher escalation helps the seller/lender's lifetime economics and does nothing for your Year 1.
